How to Download a TikTok Live Replay

Save TikTok LIVE replays and clips before they disappear.

TikTok LIVE replays are easy to miss: they stay up for a limited time, and then they are gone. If you hosted the live, or you want to keep a replay you watched, you need to act quickly and know which options actually work. This guide covers saving your own replay, saving someone else's, and what to do when the replay has already expired.

Save your own replay

After your LIVE ends, TikTok usually offers a replay in your profile or in LIVE Center. From there you can watch it and, in many regions, download it to your device. Do this promptly, because replays are typically available for only a limited period, often around 90 days, and then they are removed.

Download a replay you watched

If the creator has left the replay public, copy its link and try a downloader that supports LIVE replays. Not all tools do, so you may need to try more than one. If the replay is a short clip rather than a full stream, a standard video downloader is more likely to work.

Record the live as it happens

The most reliable way to keep a live is to screen-record it while it is happening, using your phone's built-in screen recorder. This captures exactly what you see, including comments if they are on screen. Start the recorder before the live begins, and make sure you have enough storage for a long recording.

What to do when it is gone

Once a replay expires, it cannot be recovered from TikTok. If the creator re-uploads highlights as a normal video, you can download that instead. Otherwise, check whether the creator posted the live to another platform, and ask them politely if they can share a copy.

Putting it into practice

Start with a single, short example and work through the steps above from beginning to end. Do not try to do everything at once, and do not use an important or irreplaceable video for your first attempt. Once the first try works, repeat the process with a second example, and you will find the steps become familiar quickly. Keep your files organised as you go, give them clear names, and check the finished result on the device where you plan to use it, whether that is your phone, your computer, or a tablet.

Common mistakes to avoid

The most common mistake is rushing: pasting the wrong link, picking the lowest quality, or downloading from the first site that appears in a search without checking whether it is trustworthy. The second is ignoring storage and file names, which leaves you with a folder full of files called download-1 and download-2 that you cannot tell apart. The third is forgetting the creator: always credit the person who made the original, keep personal copies personal, and ask before you reuse someone else's work in public.
