How to Save TikTok Videos for Offline Viewing

Keep your favourite TikTok videos available without internet, on any device.

Offline viewing is one of the best reasons to download a TikTok video: on a plane, on a train with poor signal, or in a place with expensive data. This guide shows how to build a small offline collection that actually works when you need it, and how to keep it organised so you can find a clip in seconds.

Download before you travel

Save the videos you want before you leave Wi-Fi. Downloading on a fast connection is quicker and cheaper than using mobile data. Make a list of the clips you want, and download them in one session so you are not rushing at the airport.

Organise by topic

Create folders for the topics you save most, such as recipes, workouts, travel, or funny clips. Clear folder names save time later, especially when you have dozens of videos. On a phone, use albums in Photos or folders in Files; on a computer, use a simple folder structure.

Check playback offline

Before you travel, switch your phone to aeroplane mode and try playing a few saved videos. This confirms they are truly on your device and not just streamed. If a video will not play offline, it may be a link or a preview rather than a downloaded file, so download it again.

Manage storage

Videos take up space, so check your storage regularly and delete clips you no longer need. A one-minute HD video can be 10 megabytes or more, so a hundred clips can fill a gigabyte. Back up favourites to a computer or cloud drive, then remove them from your phone to free space.

Putting it into practice

Start with a single, short example and work through the steps above from beginning to end. Do not try to do everything at once, and do not use an important or irreplaceable video for your first attempt. Once the first try works, repeat the process with a second example, and you will find the steps become familiar quickly. Keep your files organised as you go, give them clear names, and check the finished result on the device where you plan to use it, whether that is your phone, your computer, or a tablet.

Common mistakes to avoid

The most common mistake is rushing: pasting the wrong link, picking the lowest quality, or downloading from the first site that appears in a search without checking whether it is trustworthy. The second is ignoring storage and file names, which leaves you with a folder full of files called download-1 and download-2 that you cannot tell apart. The third is forgetting the creator: always credit the person who made the original, keep personal copies personal, and ask before you reuse someone else's work in public.
