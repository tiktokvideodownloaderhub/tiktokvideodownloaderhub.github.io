How to Download TikTok Photo Slideshows

Save TikTok photo slideshows and their images, step by step.

TikTok is not only videos: many creators post photo slideshows, a series of still images set to music that you swipe through. Saving one is trickier than saving a video, because there is no single video file to download. This guide explains how to save the images, the audio, and the whole slideshow in a usable form.

Understand the format

A TikTok slideshow is a set of images with a sound track, presented as a video-like post. When you download it through a video downloader, you may get a video version of the slideshow, or just the images, depending on the tool. Check what the tool offers before you download.

Save the images one by one

The simplest method is to screenshot each image as you swipe through the slideshow. On iPhone, press the side button and volume up; on Android, press power and volume down. Screenshots are quick, but they include the TikTok interface, so crop the image afterwards to remove the buttons and captions.

Download the slideshow as a video

Some downloaders return the slideshow as an MP4 video, which preserves the timing and the music. This is the best option if you want to rewatch it as the creator intended. Look for a downloader that explicitly supports photo posts, and check the preview to confirm it captured all the images.

Save the music separately

If you like the sound, use an audio downloader with the same link to save the MP3. You can then pair the images and the audio in a video editor. Always credit the creator and the musician if you share the result, and keep it for personal use unless you have permission.

Putting it into practice

Start with a single, short example and work through the steps above from beginning to end. Do not try to do everything at once, and do not use an important or irreplaceable video for your first attempt. Once the first try works, repeat the process with a second example, and you will find the steps become familiar quickly. Keep your files organised as you go, give them clear names, and check the finished result on the device where you plan to use it, whether that is your phone, your computer, or a tablet.

Common mistakes to avoid

The most common mistake is rushing: pasting the wrong link, picking the lowest quality, or downloading from the first site that appears in a search without checking whether it is trustworthy. The second is ignoring storage and file names, which leaves you with a folder full of files called download-1 and download-2 that you cannot tell apart. The third is forgetting the creator: always credit the person who made the original, keep personal copies personal, and ask before you reuse someone else's work in public.
