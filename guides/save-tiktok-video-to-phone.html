How to Save a TikTok Video to Your Phone

Save TikTok videos to iPhone or Android for offline viewing, with or without the app.

Your phone is the easiest place to save a TikTok video, because the app already has a built-in save button for many clips. When that button is missing, a link-based downloader fills the gap. This guide walks through both routes, so you can keep the videos you like even when you are offline or the original post is deleted.

Use the built-in save button

Tap Share on the video, then look for Save Video. If it appears, the clip downloads straight to your camera roll or gallery. This is the fastest option, and it is also the one the creator has allowed. If the button is greyed out or missing, the creator has turned downloads off for that video.

Copy the link instead

Tap Share, then Copy Link, and paste the link into a downloader in your browser. Choose the video quality you want and tap Download. On iPhone, Safari may ask where to save the file; choose Downloads in Files, then move it to Photos if you want it in your gallery.

Find the saved video

On iPhone, open Photos and check Recents, or open Files and check Downloads. On Android, open your gallery app or the Files app and look in the Download folder. If you cannot find it, search your files for the video title or sort by date, with the newest first.

Fix common problems

If a download fails, check your internet connection, free up storage, and update the TikTok app and your browser. Some older phones struggle with very long videos, so try a shorter clip first to confirm the method works. Never install an app that asks for your TikTok password just to download a video.

Putting it into practice

Start with a single, short example and work through the steps above from beginning to end. Do not try to do everything at once, and do not use an important or irreplaceable video for your first attempt. Once the first try works, repeat the process with a second example, and you will find the steps become familiar quickly. Keep your files organised as you go, give them clear names, and check the finished result on the device where you plan to use it, whether that is your phone, your computer, or a tablet.

Common mistakes to avoid

The most common mistake is rushing: pasting the wrong link, picking the lowest quality, or downloading from the first site that appears in a search without checking whether it is trustworthy. The second is ignoring storage and file names, which leaves you with a folder full of files called download-1 and download-2 that you cannot tell apart. The third is forgetting the creator: always credit the person who made the original, keep personal copies personal, and ask before you reuse someone else's work in public.
